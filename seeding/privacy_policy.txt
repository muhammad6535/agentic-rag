Enterprise Data Privacy and Security Policy

Version 3.2 | Effective January 2026

1. Purpose and Scope

This policy establishes the data privacy and security requirements for all enterprise systems, applications, and data processing activities. It applies to all employees, contractors, and third-party vendors who access or process company data.

2. Data Classification

All data must be classified into one of four categories:

Public Data: Information that can be freely shared with the public, including marketing materials and press releases.

Internal Data: Information intended for internal use only, such as internal policies, organizational charts, and non-sensitive operational data.

Confidential Data: Sensitive business information including financial data, customer information, trade secrets, and strategic plans. Access must be restricted on a need-to-know basis.

Restricted Data: Highly sensitive information including personally identifiable information (PII), protected health information (PHI), payment card data, and authentication credentials. This data requires the highest level of protection including encryption at rest and in transit, strict access controls, and comprehensive audit logging.

3. Access Control Requirements

All systems must implement role-based access control (RBAC) with the principle of least privilege. User authentication must use strong password policies with bcrypt hashing or equivalent. Multi-factor authentication is required for all systems containing confidential or restricted data.

Access reviews must be conducted quarterly for all systems containing restricted data, and annually for all other systems. Inactive user accounts must be disabled after 90 days of inactivity.

4. Data Encryption Standards

All data in transit must be encrypted using TLS 1.2 or higher. Data at rest must be encrypted using AES-256 encryption. Encryption keys must be managed through a centralized key management system with automatic rotation every 90 days.

Database encryption requirements include: transparent data encryption for all production databases, column-level encryption for sensitive fields such as social security numbers and payment card data, and encrypted backups stored in geographically separate locations.

5. Audit and Monitoring

Comprehensive audit logging is required for all systems processing confidential or restricted data. Audit logs must capture: user identity, timestamp, action performed, data accessed, source IP address, and outcome of the action.

Audit logs must be retained for a minimum of one year for general systems and seven years for systems processing restricted data. Logs must be tamper-proof and stored in a separate system from the systems being monitored.

Automated monitoring and alerting must be configured for: repeated failed login attempts, unauthorized access attempts, data exfiltration patterns, and privilege escalation events.

6. Incident Response

Security incidents must be reported to the security team within one hour of discovery. The incident response process includes: containment of the threat, eradication of the cause, recovery of affected systems, and post-incident analysis.

Data breaches involving restricted data must be reported to affected individuals within 72 hours as required by GDPR and other applicable regulations. A breach notification must include: description of the incident, types of data affected, timeline of the breach, and mitigation steps taken.

7. Vendor and Third-Party Security

All vendors must undergo a security assessment before being granted access to company systems. Vendor assessments include: review of security certifications, penetration test results, data handling practices, and incident response capabilities.

Vendors processing restricted data must sign data processing agreements and demonstrate compliance with applicable regulations. Regular audits of vendor security practices must be conducted annually.

8. Compliance and Regulatory Requirements

The enterprise must comply with all applicable data protection regulations including GDPR, CCPA, HIPAA, and SOC 2. Compliance is monitored through automated controls, periodic assessments, and external audits.

GDPR requirements include: data protection impact assessments for high-risk processing activities, appointment of a data protection officer, maintenance of records of processing activities, and support for data subject rights including access, rectification, erasure, and portability.

9. Training and Awareness

All employees must complete data privacy and security training annually. Training covers: data classification and handling procedures, phishing awareness, password security, incident reporting procedures, and regulatory compliance requirements.

Role-specific training is required for employees handling restricted data, including developers, system administrators, and data processors.

10. Policy Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment and legal prosecution. Managers are responsible for ensuring their teams comply with this policy. The Data Protection Officer is responsible for policy maintenance and interpretation.
